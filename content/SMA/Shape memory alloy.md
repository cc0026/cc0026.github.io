## 1. Definition

A shape memory alloy (SMA) is an alloy that can be deformed in a low-temperature state and recover its pre-deformed shape when heated. The memorized geometry can be modified by fixing the desired geometry and applying a thermal treatment; for example, a wire can be trained to memorize the shape of a coil spring.

SMAs are also known as memory metals, memory alloys, smart metals, smart alloys, or muscle wires.

## 2. Materials and Phases

The two most prevalent shape memory alloys are copper–aluminium–nickel and nickel–titanium (NiTi). SMAs can also be produced by alloying zinc, copper, gold, and iron. Iron-based and copper-based SMAs, including Fe–Mn–Si, Cu–Zn–Al, and Cu–Al–Ni, are commercially available and generally cheaper than NiTi. NiTi-based SMAs are preferred for many applications because of their stability, practicability, and thermo-mechanical performance.

SMAs can be described in terms of two phases and three crystal-structural states: twinned martensite, detwinned martensite, and austenite. Their thermo-mechanical behavior is governed by transformations between austenite and martensite.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> SMAs memorize their shape in the **austenite phase**.

> [!CAUTION]
> **Austenite** is the high-temperature, high-symmetry parent phase, whereas **martensite** is the low-temperature, lower-symmetry product phase. They arise through a **diffusionless, displacive martensitic transformation**, in which atoms shift collectively without long-range diffusion.

## 3. Thermal Transformation

During cooling, NiTi transforms from austenite to martensite. The transformation begins below the martensitic start temperature, $M_s$, and finishes at the martensitic finish temperature, $M_f$.

During heating, the reverse transformation from martensite to austenite begins at the austenitic start temperature, $A_s$, and finishes at the austenitic finish temperature, $A_f$.

The temperature difference between the forward and reverse transformations is discussed in [[Hysteresis]].

## 4. Mechanical Behavior

Applying a mechanical load to martensite re-orients the crystals through detwinning. This produces a deformation that is not recovered immediately after the load is released. Detwinning begins at a stress $\sigma_s$ and ends at $\sigma_f$; above $\sigma_f$, martensite exhibits only elastic behavior as long as the load remains below the yield stress. The deformation produced by detwinning is recovered after heating the material into the austenite phase.

The austenite-to-martensite transformation can also occur at approximately constant temperature when a sufficiently large mechanical load is applied. Releasing the load reverses this stress-induced transformation.

## 5. Comparison with Steel

The transition between martensite and austenite in an SMA depends on temperature and stress rather than time in the usual diffusion-controlled sense, because the transformation is diffusionless. This reversible transformation gives SMAs their characteristic functional properties.

Martensite can also form from austenite in rapidly cooled carbon steel, but the transformation in steel is not reversible in the same way. Carbon steel therefore does not exhibit the same shape-memory behavior.
